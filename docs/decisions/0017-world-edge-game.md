# 0017: World edge in the game

Status: Accepted

## Context

ADR 0015 makes the pipeline write the world edge to `boundary.json`: roadblocks, land and water fences, the cliff, the void trigger prisms and the spawn area. The game has to turn that into barriers the player can jump but not walk through, a volume that counts as falling off the world, and a death and respawn that replaces ADR 0016's temporary fall respawn. Every rule has to hold for later bodies (vehicles, pedestrians, police) too. Spec: `docs/specs/world-edge-game.md` (decided 2026-11-04).

## Decision

- **Barriers.** Three types built in code from primitives, no assets: `Roadblock` (light red-and-white boards on stands), `LandFence` (posts with barbed-wire strands) and `WaterFence` (posts with a line on floats; at terrain height until water surfaces exist, ADR 0015). `MapLoader` places one item per `boundary.json` roadblock or fence under `MapLoader/Edge/Barriers`. The piece layout is engine-free (`Roadman.Core.Edge.BarrierLayout`). Rules for every item:
  - no piece more than **0.9 m** above its base (the fence line from `start` to `end`, or the ground under a roadblock stand), so the 1.3 m jump apex of ADR 0016 clears it;
  - nothing reaches more than **1.0 m** outward of the boundary (`void.inner_offset_m`), so no item starts inside the void trigger, and the inward half stays under `building_clearance_m` (2.0 m);
  - the items' collision covers the ring with no gap over 0.1 m; fence-to-fence joints share one post (each fence owns the post at its end, and its start post only after a roadblock);
  - roadblock stands stand on the highest terrain or road hit under them, not on the `start`–`end` line, which is up to 3.4 m off on Somero's road 52.
- **Static until hit.** Each item is one `StaticBody3D` with its collision shapes; the visuals are batched in one `MultiMeshInstance3D` per piece mesh and barrier type for the whole map. Only vehicles (bodies implementing `IBarrierBreaker`) break an item, at `BreakSpeed` (3 m/s, `BarrierSettings.BreakingSpeed`) or more, into loose `RigidBody3D` pieces; the player on foot never does. Broken items are rebuilt and loose pieces freed when the player respawns and on a map reload.
- **Collision layers.** Loose barrier pieces are on their own layer, `CollisionLayers.Barrier` (layer 3), which the void trigger monitors; unbroken static items are on `CollisionLayers.BarrierStatic`, which it doesn't (amended 2026-11-04, part B: monitoring the 776 static items cost about 0.1 ms per frame headless on Somero, about ten times the split's cost, and a static item never moves into the void). The player's mask is the map plus both barrier layers; the camera's spring arm and the map rays keep mask 1, so the camera passes through barriers. Layer 4 (`CollisionLayers.Vehicle`) is reserved for vehicles.
- **Void trigger.** One `Area3D` (`MapLoader/Edge/VoidTrigger`) with one convex shape per prism, monitoring the player, barrier and vehicle layers (not the map). A body entering it is handled with the outward direction at its position (`EdgeGeometry.OutwardAt`): the player starts the death sequence, a body implementing `IVoidFaller` gets `EnterVoid(outward)` and handles its own fall (the hook for vehicles and characters), any other `RigidBody3D` falls under `VoidPull` and is freed below the trigger's floor, past the safety net or after `MaxFallTime`.
- **Safety net.** Replaces the temporary fall respawn of ADR 0016. Each physics frame, the player and the bodies `World` tracks are checked against `cliff.bottom_y − SafetyNetMargin` (20 m, the trigger's floor on Somero); a body below it that isn't falling yet is handled as a void entry. It catches gaps in collision, teleports and bodies launched over the trigger's top.
- **Death sequence.** The state machine is engine-free (`Roadman.Core.Edge.DeathSequence`: `Alive`, `Falling`, `DeathScreen`, `Respawning`, `Failed`). `PlayerState.Die()` runs at the moment the player enters the void, after the losses (money and gear ids) are recorded. During the fall the player's input is off, its collision mask is cleared (its layer stays, so the trigger and the net still see it) and gravity is replaced by `VoidPull` (outward 4 m/s², down at project gravity); a `DeathCamera` pulls back from the player camera to look down past the edge. A death screen follows (any key, gamepad button or mouse click dismisses it after 2 s, else it closes after 5 s; amended 2026-11-04, part B), then the player respawns through the existing spawn draw (`--seed` repeats it). A respawn with no passing draw pushes the spawn error and frees the player. The debug camera toggle does nothing outside `Alive`.
- **Death screen wording** (English, decided): "The Earth is flat", "You fell off the edge of the world", and `Money lost: <amount> · Gear lost: <ids or none>`. Plain text on a dark fade until a UI style exists.
- **Space backdrop.** A procedural sky shader (`materials/sky/space_sky.gdshader`), no assets: the day sky above the horizon and a starfield below it. The radiance pass keeps the old ground colour below the horizon, so the ambient and reflected light the town gets from the sky don't change.
- **Budget.** The world edge (barriers, trigger, safety net and backdrop) costs under 1 ms per frame on the full Somero pack: the headless cost (mean frame time at one physics step per frame) plus the windowed render-time cost (CPU and GPU), each the median of 3 alternating runs with minus without the world edge (amended 2026-11-04, part B).

## Consequences

- Vehicles, pedestrians and police implement `IVoidFaller` (and vehicles `IBarrierBreaker`) in their own specs, put their bodies on layers the trigger monitors, and register with `World` for the safety net. A vehicle carrying the player hands the player to the death sequence.
- Water fences and their criteria change when water surfaces are added.
- Barrier tuning (sizes, `PostSpacing`, `BreakSpeed`, the pull and camera values) is in exports and recorded after the playtest.
- `World.PlayerRespawned` now means a respawn after a death.
