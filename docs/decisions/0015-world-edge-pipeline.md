# 0015: World edge pipeline

Status: Accepted

## Context

The map needs a closed, physical edge: roadblocks where roads leave the playable area, fences along the rest, and a cliff just outside them, so the world reads as a slab floating in space. The game also needs a void trigger volume, a spawn area for respawns and the traffic role of every road end at the edge. All of it has to come from map data, with no map-specific code (ADR 0002), and be checked on every build. Somero's build area cuts its lake and 31 buildings at the bbox edge, so a custom boundary polygon is needed too. Spec: `docs/specs/world-edge-pipeline.md` (with its "Amendments"). Pipeline stage 10 in `docs/architecture.md`.

## Decision

- **Pack file `boundary.json` v1.** Always written, in Godot local metres, sorted and rounded to 1 µm so two builds from the same input are byte-identical. A pydantic model with a committed JSON Schema (`tools/mapgen/src/mapgen/build/schemas/boundary-v1.schema.json`), validated on every write; unknown keys are errors. Fields: `schema_version`, `map_id`, `area`, `boundary` (ring, length, source `area`, `custom` or `test_area`), `roadblocks`, `fences`, `cliff` (`ledge_m`, `bottom_y`), `void_trigger` (`bottom_y`, `top_y`, convex prisms), `spawn_area` (polygon, open ground and its area) and `water_crossings`. Not listed in the manifest, which stays at version 1. The ring is counter-clockwise from its lowest-x, then lowest-n vertex; every ring item has `s_start` and `s_end`.
- **Map file `markers.json` v1.** `maps/<map-id>/markers.json`: `schema_version` 1 and `spawn_area_wgs84`; unknown keys are errors; committed schema `markers-v1.schema.json`; validated before the load stage. A missing file fails the build, because respawn needs a spawn area in every pack. Parking spots and gameplay points of interest come with their own specs and a version change.
- **Config.** An optional `world_edge` block in `map.yaml`, validated by `MapConfig` (unknown keys are errors). Every key has a pipeline default, so a map lists only what it overrides. The defaults and their reasons (from the spec's "Config" table, decided 2026-11-02; the Somero figures are for boundary B):
  - `building_clearance_m` 2.0: more than the inward half-depth of a barrier, so no fence post or roadblock stands in a wall. B was routed at this value (closest building 2.12 m).
  - `fence.max_segment_m` 20.0: on B, with the 0.25 m deviation, about 770 segments, against about 1,210 at 10 m; at 50 m about 570, but long straight segments would then rely on the deviation split alone.
  - `fence.max_terrain_deviation_m` 0.25: the barrier must stay low enough to jump over on foot. Where the terrain dips under a segment, the fence's top is that much higher above the ground, so this caps what the pipeline adds to the game's barrier height. On B, 0.25 m gives about 770 segments, 0.1 m about 1,140 and 0.5 m about 660.
  - `roadblock.clearance_m` 1.0: covers the road shoulder, so a car can't pass between a roadblock end and the fence.
  - `roadblock.merge_distance_m` 15.0: decided 2026-11-01; the spec gives no measured reason.
  - `cliff.ledge_m` 2.0: room for the barrier's outward half and the start of the void trigger (1.0 m) on solid ground, so the drop reads as "just outside the barrier" and a player who jumps the fence lands on the ledge inside the trigger.
  - `cliff.depth_m` 30.0: Somero's boundary runs from 81.8 to 133.4 m, so `bottom_y` is 51.8 and the slab is 30 to 81.6 m thick, a visible slab from below in the death sequence. A visual value, checked by the screenshot (criterion 20).
  - `void.inner_offset_m` 1.0: beyond the outward half of a barrier, so barrier pieces at rest never start inside the trigger.
  - `void.width_m` 20.0: many times what a body moves in one physics tick (0.83 m at 50 m/s at 60 Hz), so nothing passes through the band between two ticks.
  - `void.top_margin_m` 20.0: covers a jump or a vehicle airborne at the edge.
  - `spawn.obstacle_buffer_m` 2.0: keeps a respawned player off roads and out of walls, with room for the drop onto the ground.
  - `spawn.min_open_area_m2` 500: enough room for random respawn points to differ (about 22 × 22 m).
  - `spawn.min_clear_radius_m` 5.0: at least one spot about a car length across, so the area isn't only thin slivers between obstacles.
  - `spawn.min_boundary_distance_m` 10.0: well clear of the barrier, the ledge (2 m) and the void trigger (from 1 m outside), so a respawn never lands at the drop.
- **Polygons in WGS84.** `world_edge.boundary_wgs84` and `spawn_area_wgs84` are lists of `{lat, lon}` points without a repeated first point, projected and moved to local coordinates like every other input. Boundary points up to 0.05 m outside the rounded build area (`BOUNDARY_SNAP_M`; 7-decimal rounding moves points by a few mm) are clamped onto its edge, points further out fail validation, and local points are rounded to 1 µm, so `boundary.json` holds the exact polygon the build cut with. A polygon that isn't a single valid, simple polygon of at least 3 points inside the build area fails validation before the pack is touched.
- **The boundary.** The build area rectangle by default, the custom polygon in a full build that has one, and the test-area square in every test build (whatever the map says). With a custom polygon, everything the build area clips is clipped by the polygon instead: building and POI centroids, road ways by line (`area_edge` nodes on the polygon), road and path surfaces, and the terrain mesh, cut along it (`build/terrain/clip.py`). The tile grid and tile bounds stay the build area's; a tile is written when any of its terrain lies inside the boundary.
- **Water.** Water areas come from the MML Topographic Database when a source provides the `water_areas` capability (`mml_topographic` reads `jarvi` and `virtavesialue`, and the water lines `virtavesikapea`; a missing table is an error naming it, an empty one is fine), and from OSM water areas otherwise. So the test-area fixture's GPKG carries the three tables `jarvi`, `virtavesialue` and `virtavesikapea`, empty for Somero (no MML water meets the test area), because the build needs them. In OSM mode, a water relation the load stage left out within `cliff.ledge_m + void.width_m` of the boundary fails the build. The parts of the boundary inside water are water crossings and get water fences; a line-only waterway gets a land fence and a report entry.
- **Water fences at terrain height** (`Heightfield.sample`) until water surfaces exist (stage 5); then they move to the water surface.
- **Roadblocks.** Every `area_edge` node is in exactly one roadblock. A roadblock spans the boundary stretch of the road or path surface containing its node, plus `roadblock.clearance_m` on each side. Road and path runs ending at an `area_edge` cut reach the boundary across their whole width (ADR 0012, amendment of 2026-11-02), so the span covers the road where it crosses. Roadblocks whose fence gap is under 15 m are merged, until none is. A road surface touching the boundary without crossing it gets no roadblock; the fence runs over it and the report lists it. A roadblock sits at terrain + `surface_offset_m`, or, when one of its nodes ends a bridge deck edge, at the highest of those nodes' graph heights (the deck), with `over_water` when its span lies in a water crossing.
- **Traffic roles in `boundary.json`.** Each roadblock node has role `spawn_despawn` (drivable edge) or `turn_around` (walk-only); `road_graph.json` stays at version 1.
- **Fences** cover the rest of the ring, split at ring vertices and water crossing ends, at most `fence.max_segment_m` long, and land fences until the terrain is within `fence.max_terrain_deviation_m` of their line. Their ends are on the terrain. Roadblocks and fences tile the ring exactly (closed loop within 1 mm).
- **Buildings never cross the boundary.** A building crosses it when part of its footprint lies outside or within `building_clearance_m` of it. With the default rectangle (and the test-area square), crossing buildings are dropped before tiling and listed in the report. A custom polygon must be routed around buildings: a kept building crossing it fails the build, as does a building whose centroid is outside but whose footprint reaches inside (intersection area > 0). A building with its centroid outside isn't in the pack at all (no mesh, collision, `buildings.json` entry or POI link), so the clearance, which keeps barrier pieces out of walls, applies only to kept buildings, measured from the inside. A pack check makes sure of it: every `bld_` tile node (mesh or collision) belongs to a building in `buildings.json`, so a building left out has neither. On Somero's B, 7-decimal rounding brings 42 outside buildings a few mm under 2 m and 2 MML records sit at 1.81 and 1.72 m; none of them is in the pack.
- **Void trigger.** Convex prisms covering the band from `void.inner_offset_m` to `void.inner_offset_m + void.width_m` outside the boundary, corners included, clipped to stay outside it. Their footprints are checked to be valid convex quadrilaterals, and 10,000 sampled band points must lie in a prism. `top_y` is the highest boundary height plus `void.top_margin_m`, `bottom_y` the cliff's `bottom_y` minus `void.width_m`. The inner offset sets a limit for the game: barrier scenes reach at most 1.0 m outward from the boundary.
- **The cliff and bottom cap are tile nodes.** Each written tile the boundary passes through has `edge_cliff-col` (visual and collision: the ledge, `cliff.ledge_m` wide outward from the boundary, and the face down to `bottom_y` = lowest boundary height − `cliff.depth_m`), and every written tile has `edge_bottom` (visual only, the flat cap under the slab, facing down). A tile the boundary passes through is a written tile whose bounds meet the ring in a straight stretch longer than 1 mm on the stretch's playable side: the stretch's midpoint, moved into the playable area, lies in the tile's bounds. So a stretch lying on a grid line between two tiles (or on the build area's edge) counts only for the tile on its playable side. The build cuts the ring at its vertices and grid crossings and gives each stretch the grid cell of its midpoint moved 0.1 mm inward (`stretch_tile`); `check_cliff` finds the tiles from the manifest's tile bounds alone, moving the midpoint 1 mm, and requires exactly these tiles to have `edge_cliff-col`. Both are flat-shaded (faces share no vertices; each vertex normal is its face normal), low-poly (planar quads, one per boundary piece between consecutive fence or roadblock ends, split at ring vertices and tile edges), without UVs, with one plain glTF material `edge_cliff` that the game can replace. Implementation choices (spec amendments, slice (c)):
  - The ledge's inner edge runs straight between its item's end heights, so it is at the fence and roadblock heights.
  - Convex corners are mitred; a mitre reaching more than 2 ledge widths from its corner is clipped square to the bisector there. Concave corners are split along the bisector. Overlaps at short segments and narrow inlets go to the nearest piece or corner (the one whose ring geometry is nearest to the cell's representative point), so the ledge never overlaps itself or the playable area. Where another covering piece or corner, of a different height or tile, is nearer at one of a cell's vertices (in an inlet narrower than `cliff.ledge_m`, one cell spans both walls), the cell is split where the two are equally near and the ledge is noded again, up to 4 times, so each fence end has ledge vertices at its own height.
  - Vertical walls close every height step: seams between pieces (a roadblock on the ground sits `surface_offset_m` above the fence ends beside it, 0.05 m on Somero; deck roadblocks sit at deck height), and a skirt on the ring from the ledge down (or up) to the terrain mesh's own edge, so the terrain meets the cliff mesh within 1 mm although a fence line is up to 0.25 m from the terrain. The skirt was the user's choice (2026-11-02) over no skirt (slits up to 0.25 m) and a ledge following every terrain vertex; it is the one part of the cliff finer than the fence segmentation.
  - A piece belongs to the tile on the playable side of its stretch of the ring, overhanging the tile's bounds like a building. A piece or cap part whose tile isn't written goes to the nearest written tile that the boundary passes through.
  - The bottom cap is the playable area plus the ledge, cut into the tile grid cells; the outer cells reach past the build area's edge, so the cap also lies under the ledge where it overhangs the build area.
  - The terrain edge along the ring (for the skirt) comes from the terrain mesh edges on the ring. The terrain always reaches the boundary, so a ring stretch longer than 1 mm without one fails the build instead of being interpolated over.
- **Spawn area check.** The spawn polygon must lie inside the test area (so inside both builds' boundaries). Open ground = the polygon minus buildings, water and road, path and deck surfaces grown by `spawn.obstacle_buffer_m`, and minus everything within `spawn.min_boundary_distance_m` of the boundary; it needs `spawn.min_open_area_m2` and room for a disc of `spawn.min_clear_radius_m`. `boundary.json` carries the polygon and the open ground, so the game draws respawn points from it directly.
- **Pack checks.** Every rule above that the pack guarantees is checked on the staged pack in every build, including `--release` (`check_world_edge`, `check_cliff`, `check_custom_boundary` in `build/pack_checks.py`). `check_cliff` (criterion 14), from `boundary.json`, the manifest and the tiles' meshes:
  - exactly the tiles the boundary passes through (found from geometry, as above) have `edge_cliff-col`, and every written tile has `edge_bottom`; every ledge stretch on the ring lies on the playable side within its own tile's bounds (or of no written tile, for a moved piece);
  - faces share no vertices, every vertex normal is its face's unit normal (the three alike; faces over 0.01 m² within 0.001 of the float32 face normal), the cap faces down, and triangles sharing an edge and a normal are planar (0.2 mm);
  - the ledge's inner edges cover the ring with no gap over 1 mm, every roadblock and fence end has a ledge vertex at its height (1 mm), and no ledge point lies more than 1 mm inside the playable area;
  - the lowest cliff vertex and every cap vertex are at `bottom_y` (1 mm);
  - the caps cover the playable area plus the ledge, within each written tile's bounds and as a whole, without overlapping (0.2 mm times the outline length, for float32 positions);
  - the face reaches `bottom_y` all along the cap's outline (a sample every 0.5 m within 1 mm of a vertical face's bottom edge).
- **Somero.** Boundary B ("west notch", 162 points in `maps/somero/map.yaml` since the 2026-11-04 amendment, 184 before): the build area with notches around edge buildings and the west edge stepped in to x −1103 across Kirkkojärvi, so the lake stays inside with a 255.8 m water fence. Ring 10,330 m (10,560 m before), 41 roadblocks (34 drivable), 718 land (747 before) and 17 water fences. Spawn area S1 (`maps/somero/markers.json`), an open patch inside the test area, 5,765 m² with 5,308 m² open ground, so one spawn area serves both builds and the test area and CI fixture stay as they are.
- **Screenshot.** The camera preset `world-edge` (`maps/somero/camera_presets.json`) looks along B's north edge at the road 52 roadblock on Jokioistentie, from outside the build area. Derived from the full build's `boundary.json`: the roadblock's `position` P = (−325.4, 87.3, −1171.0), `outward` (0, 0, −1) (north), and its side direction from `end` to `start` (+X). The camera is 40 m along +X and 30 m outward from P, 12 m above the ledge: (−285.4, 99.3, −1201.0). It looks at the middle of the ledge beyond P, 15 m below the ledge, about 42% of the way down the 35.5 m face: P + 1 m outward (half of `cliff.ledge_m`) at y 72.3, that is (−325.4, 72.3, −1172.0). The look-at is on the ledge rather than at its outer edge, so the screenshot tool's downward ray there (see below) hits the ledge without depending on float rounding at the edge. From there a 70° view holds the road up to the ledge, the ledge and the face down to `bottom_y` (51.8). Heights are absolute (`ground_relative: false`), because there is no terrain under the camera. It doesn't follow the ledge if the DEM or `cliff.depth_m` changes. The screenshot tool returns N/A (exit 2) for any preset where a downward ray at the look-at x/z hits neither the terrain (`map_terrain`) nor the world-edge cliff (`map_edge`) (decided by the user), so on a test-area pack it is N/A, as ADR 0008 expects for presets outside the area.

## Consequences

- The game spec that follows reads `boundary.json` for the barrier scenes, the void `Area3D`, respawn and traffic spawning; its barrier scenes must stay within 1.0 m outward of the boundary.
- A map without a `world_edge` block gets the defaults and the build area rectangle; a map needs `markers.json` to build at all.
- The custom polygon must be routed around buildings by hand (or by a script, as B was); the build names every building it crosses.
- Cost (full Somero build): the world edge stage about 0.8 s, the cliff 4.1 s, the cliff pack check 0.3 s. On B the cliff has 841 pieces (69 mitred, 18 clipped and 97 concave corners) and 18 cell splits between pieces or corners equally near, and adds 25,440 triangles in `edge_cliff-col` (ledge 2,742, face 2,036, seam 639, skirt 20,023) in the 36 tiles the boundary passes through, and 1,143 in `edge_bottom` over all 100 tiles. The skirt is most of it, because it follows every terrain edge vertex on the ring; a coarser terrain later makes it smaller.
- Water fences and their criteria change when water surfaces are added.
- The ledge follows the fence and roadblock lines, not the terrain, so at the edge of the playable area the ground steps by up to 0.25 m (the skirt) onto the ledge.
