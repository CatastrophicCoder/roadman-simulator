# 0012: Roads, bridges and the road graph

Status: Accepted

## Context

The map needs road surfaces that cars drive on and a road graph for traffic AI, pedestrian AI, police, routing and the minimap (project outline, pipeline stages 4 and 7). Both come from OSM `highway` ways, which are sparsely tagged: in Somero, 320 of 498 ways have no `maxspeed`, 465 no `lanes` and 496 no `width`. Defaults differ by country, so they can't live in the pipeline (ADR 0002). The terrain is a mesh at DEM resolution (ADR 0004 is still open), and roads must neither sink into it nor float. Spec: `docs/specs/roads-and-road-graph.md`.

## Decision

- **Config.** All road defaults are in an optional `roads` block in `maps/<map-id>/map.yaml`, validated by `MapConfig`: `surface_offset_m`, `default_speed_kmh`, `bridge` (deck thickness, railing size, minimum clearance, the waterway values that trigger inferred bridges) and `classes`, one entry per road class with `width_m`, `lanes`, `drivable`, `walkable` and an optional `speed_kmh`. A `highway` value without a class entry is skipped. A map without the block builds without road meshes and writes a `road_graph.json` with empty `nodes` and `edges`, so every pack has the file and a missing file is always an error. OSM values always win over the defaults, and every edge records whether each value came from OSM or a default.
- **Clipping by line.** A way is kept when its line crosses the build area, however few of its nodes lie inside, and is cut at the edge with `area_edge` nodes. The world-edge feature needs these ends for roadblocks.
- **One graph.** Roads and paths are in one graph, and each edge is flagged drivable and/or walkable. Nodes exist only at OSM nodes (way ends and nodes shared by two or more included ways) and at cuts by the build area's edge (`area_edge`, also for an OSM node lying exactly on the edge where the way continues outside), so a bridge never connects to what it crosses unless OSM gives them a shared node. Ids are stable: `osm:node/<id>`, `cut:way/<id>:<n>`, `osm:way/<id>#<n>`.
- **Name fields.** Edges carry `name`, `name_sv` and `ref` (from `name`, `name:sv` and `ref`), always present, a trimmed, NFC-normalised string or `null`, otherwise kept exactly as tagged. Later place information reuses this format.
- **Draping.** Ground road and path surfaces are cut along each tile's actual terrain mesh triangles and lie in each triangle's plane plus `surface_offset_m` (Somero: 0.05 m). The surface is therefore parallel to the terrain everywhere, whatever builds the terrain mesh. The terrain itself is never changed. `road` (drivable ways) and `path` (the rest, minus `road`) are merged per tile, so intersections don't overlap.
- **Collision.** Road, path and bridge meshes carry collision (`road-col`, `path-col`, `bridge_osm_way_<id>[_p<n>]-col`), so wheels sit on the visible surface. In the game their bodies are in the group `map_roads`, not `map_terrain`.
- **Split at tile edges.** Unlike buildings (ADR 0006), road surfaces and bridge decks are split at tile edges, with identical vertices on both sides.
- **Bridges.** A span is a chain of bridge ways joined at nodes no other way uses. Its deck's driving surface runs in a straight line between the road surface heights at its two ends, never following the terrain below, and is a low-poly slab with box railings and no supports. A span's end height is the highest terrain mesh height plus `surface_offset_m` along the deck's end edge, so the whole end edge is on or above the ground. On the road side of each deck end a ramp `bridge.ramp_length_m` long (Somero: 5 m) blends the surface from that height to terrain + offset (`max(blend, terrain + offset)`), so there is no step at the deck end and the terrain is never changed. A deck closer to the terrain than `min_clearance_m` is a warning in the build report, not a failure. Because a deck's ends always meet the ground, the check covers the deck between its first and last points that reach `min_clearance_m`; a deck that never reaches it is reported with `never_clears: true`. A node in the middle of a span that another way also uses takes the deck line's height, so every edge ending there ends exactly at the node; that way's ground surface ends with a flat cap under the deck. Tunnels are in the graph only, without a mesh.
- **Inferred bridges.** An included way without a `bridge` or `tunnel` tag that crosses a water area or a listed waterway is treated as a bridge (`bridge_source: "inferred"`) and listed under `suspected_missing_bridges` in the build report with its OSM id and position. Culverts, waterways below ground and fords don't count.
- **Pack checks.** The checks on tiles, roads, decks, ramps and terrain under decks run on the staged pack in every build, including `--release`, and a failed check fails the build. `export_glb` reads back every written mesh and fails on any difference. This followed one full build whose tile had 8 KB of zeroed terrain indices; the cause wasn't found and four rebuilds were clean.
- **Pack file.** `road_graph.json` (schema version 1, Godot axes) is defined by a pydantic model with a committed JSON Schema, validated on every write and found by its fixed name. It isn't listed in the manifest, so the manifest stays at version 1.
- **Connectivity.** The largest weakly connected drivable component must hold at least 95% of the drivable edges in a full-area build; test-area builds only report the share, because the cut at the test-area edge splits roads off.

## Consequences

- A new map sets its own road widths, speeds and flags in config, with no code change.
- When the terrain moves to a coarser low-poly grid or to Terrain3D, the draping, the offset, the road collision and the tile-edge matching need checking again.
- Deck ends sit up to the terrain's side slope higher than the road height at their centre node (the origin bridge: about 0.15–0.18 m), and the ramps take up the difference. Before the ramps, the largest step between a deck end and the road beside it in the full Somero build was 1.435 m; with them it is 2.4 mm.
- The terrain under a deck is never changed, so where it rises above a straight deck it shows through it. The build reports this per deck (`terrain_above_deck`); in Somero, 6 of 12 decks are affected, up to 0.80 m over 37 m². The clearance warning doesn't catch it, because it only checks between the first and last points that clear `min_clearance_m`.
- Streaming tiles later must load the road pieces of each tile, since roads are split.
- The road graph has real street names; how business names are shown is a separate decision.
- Building cost (2026-10-29): a full Somero build takes about 30 s, of which the roads stage is about 19 s, the pack checks 3.4 s and the read-back 0.25 s; a test-area build takes about 1.4 s.
